Privacy Policy

Ad On Group and its divisions

Last updated: 16 November 2026 Business: Ad On Group (ABN 54 808 983 598) Covers: Ad On Workforce, Ad On AI, Ad On Digital, Ad On Hold and Ad On SA

1. About this policy

This policy explains how Ad On Group and its divisions (“we”, “us”, “our”) collect, use, share and protect personal information. It applies to our websites, including adongroup.com.au and adon-ai.com.au, our AI Academy learning platform, and all of our services.

We handle personal information in line with the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and the Australian Privacy Principles. Section 11 explains how we look after the information our clients give us to deliver their services.

2. The information we collect

Depending on how you deal with us, we may collect:

  • Contact details, such as your name, email address, phone number and business name.
  • Enquiry and booking details, such as your message, the time of any call you book and your answers to booking questions.
  • Advertising details, such as the page you arrived on and, if you came from an ad, the campaign details in the link.
  • Customer and billing details, such as the services you use, invoices and payment history. Card payments are handled by our billing and payment providers.
  • Learner details, if you or your team use the AI Academy, such as your name, email address and progress through the course.
  • Website usage details, such as the pages you visit, the device and browser you use and how you found us.

We only collect sensitive information, such as health information, if you choose to give it to us or we need it to provide a service, and only with your consent.

3. How we collect it

  • From you, when you fill in a form, book a call, phone or email us, sign up to a service or use the AI Academy.
  • Automatically, through cookies and similar tools on our websites (see section 7).
  • From others, such as your employer if they enrol you in training, or a person who refers you to us.

4. How we use it

  • To reply to your enquiry and send you the information you asked for.
  • To confirm, remind you about and prepare for calls you book, including by email and text message.
  • To provide, manage and bill for our services, and to support you while you use them.
  • To understand which of our pages and ads are working, and to improve them.
  • To tell you about our services, if you have not opted out (see section 5).
  • To meet our legal obligations and protect our business.

5. Marketing emails and texts

We may send you emails or text messages about our services, offers and events. We only do this in line with the Spam Act 2003 (Cth).

You can opt out at any time by following the opt-out instructions in the email (for example, replying STOP), replying STOP to a text message, or contacting us (see section 14). Opting out of marketing does not stop messages we need to send about a booking or a service you use.

6. Advertising on Facebook and Instagram

We advertise on Facebook and Instagram, which are run by Meta. To make those ads more relevant and to measure whether they work, we may share some information with Meta:

  • Customer lists. We may upload the names, email addresses and phone numbers of people who have enquired with us, booked a call or become customers. This lets Meta show our ads to those people, leave them out of ads that are not relevant to them, or find other people with similar interests. The details are scrambled into a code (“hashed”) before they are sent. Meta uses the code only to match people to their accounts and then deletes it.
  • Results of our ads. We may tell Meta when an enquiry turns into a booking, a meeting or a sale, so we can see which ads bring in real customers. Any contact details we include are hashed in the same way.
  • The Meta Pixel. Our websites use the Meta Pixel to measure the results of our ads, including when someone sends an enquiry or books a call. Meta may also use this information to show you more relevant advertising.

If you don’t want to be included in our customer lists, contact us and we will remove you from future uploads. You can also manage the ads you see in your Meta ad preferences. Meta’s own use of information is covered by the Meta Privacy Policy.

7. Cookies and analytics

Our websites use cookies and similar tools:

  • Google Analytics, to understand how visitors use our websites.
  • The Meta Pixel, as described in section 6.
  • Microsoft Clarity, on some pages, to see how visitors scroll and click, including recordings of visits. Anything you type into our forms is hidden from these recordings.

You can block or delete cookies in your browser settings and our websites will still work. You can also opt out of Google Analytics with Google’s browser add-on.

8. Who we share it with

We share personal information only as needed to run our business and provide our services, including with:

  • Our software and service providers, including Google (Workspace, Gmail, Sheets and Analytics), Meta, Microsoft, Calendly, Formspree, ClickSend (text messages), Chargebee (billing), Vercel (website hosting) and our AI Academy learning platform.
  • Our professional advisers, such as accountants, lawyers and insurers.
  • Government bodies and others, where the law requires or allows it.

We do not sell personal information.

9. Information held or accessed overseas

  • Some of our providers, including Google, Meta, Microsoft, Calendly, Formspree, ClickSend, Chargebee and Vercel, may store or process information outside Australia, including in the United States.
  • Our team members in the Philippines and South Africa have strictly controlled access to client information, limited to what they need for their role. They do not have access to enquiry or booking information.
  • We take reasonable steps to make sure anyone handling personal information for us overseas protects it in line with the Australian Privacy Principles.

10. Keeping it safe

We take reasonable steps to protect personal information from misuse, loss and unauthorised access, change or disclosure. Access is limited to the people who need it, and our systems are protected with passwords and access controls.

We keep personal information only for as long as we need it for the purposes in this policy or the law requires, and then delete or de-identify it.

If a data breach is likely to cause serious harm, we will tell the people affected and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner, as the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme requires.

11. Information our clients give us

When a business engages us, it may give us information about its customers, staff or operations to deliver the service. When we handle that information, we:

  • Treat it as confidential and protect it against theft, loss and unauthorised access.
  • Use it only to provide the services the client has engaged us for, and follow the client’s reasonable instructions and data-handling procedures.
  • Give access only to team members who need it to do their work, and make sure they know it is confidential.
  • Comply with the Privacy Act as if we are bound by it, even where an exemption might apply.
  • Never sell, rent, data-mine or otherwise commercialise it.
  • Tell the client straight away if we become aware of a breach involving their information, and help them put it right.
  • Restore it at our own cost if we cause it to be lost or damaged.
  • Return or destroy it when the client asks or the service ends, except for a copy we need to keep for our legal or record-keeping obligations, which stays confidential.
  • Don’t announce or publicise our work with a client without their written approval.

We may share client information only with our team, with the client’s written consent, or where the law requires it. A client’s service agreement with us may include further terms, and those terms apply if they differ from this section.

12. Accessing and correcting your information

You can ask to see the personal information we hold about you, or ask us to correct it, by contacting us (see section 14). We will reply within 30 days. If we can’t give you access, we will explain why.

13. Complaints

If you are concerned about how we have handled your personal information, please contact us first so we can try to fix it. We will reply within 30 days. If you are not satisfied with our response, you can contact the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner.
